# THE BERMUDA RECLAMATION
### A setting primer for *Last Call on the S.V. Dross*

> *How the smallest sovereign nation in the history of Earth ended up running the entire human race, and why nobody is especially happy about it.*

---

## THE SHORT VERSION (for reading aloud or skimming)

Sometime in the **3470s**, the planet cleared its throat.

A seismic event of frankly unreasonable size — formally the *Atlantic Reclamation Event*, colloquially **the Great Heave** — split the seabed in the middle of the Bermuda Triangle and shoved a brand-new continent up into the sunlight. After several thousand years of swallowing aircraft, boats, and the occasional confident yachtsman, the Triangle had apparently been *saving up*.

The new landmass — **the Rise** — turned out to be packed with a rare-earth alloy you cannot make a spaceship without. Earth was, at that point, not in a position to be picky about good news. The waters had risen, the old powers had spent the previous century arguing about whose fault it was, and here was the answer to all of it, sitting in international waters, attached to the one nation small enough that nobody had ever bothered to invade it.

And so, through no plan, no war, and no merit whatsoever, the human race ended up governed by **Bermuda**.

---

## THE METAL THAT RUNS EVERYTHING

The alloy in the Rise does the one thing physics had previously refused to do cheaply: it makes spaceflight *practical*. Light, absurdly conductive, stable under stresses that turn ordinary matter into regret.

Scientists called it **unobtainium** for roughly eleven days, until a legal team pointed out that the name implied the substance was difficult to obtain, when in fact Bermuda now had a continent of the stuff and wished to sell it at scale. After a brief and expensive rebranding it became **reclaimite**, and everyone who works near it just calls it **lift**.

Every ship in human space — from the gleaming Crown frigates to the wheezing garbage barge you are about to crew — runs on lift dug out of the Rise. Which means every ship in human space is, ultimately, paying rent to Bermuda. The genius of the arrangement is that no one ever decided this. It simply accreted, the way limescale accretes, and by the time anyone thought to object the paperwork was already load-bearing.

---

## HOW A TAX HAVEN ACCIDENTALLY BECAME A GALAXY

Here is the thing about Bermuda that the rest of old Earth forgot, right up until it mattered: it had spent five hundred years being *very good at one thing.* Not mining. Not war. **Insurance.**

When the world reorganized itself around the Rise, it didn't reorganize around generals or kings. It reorganized around the people who already knew how to price risk, pool capital, and write a contract no one could escape. The **Actuariat** — the reinsurance class, the underwriters, the people who calculate exactly how likely you are to die and charge you accordingly — simply kept doing their jobs, and discovered one morning that their jobs now included *everything*.

Today the human race is governed by the **Sovereign Bermudian Reclamation Authority**, which everyone shortens to **the Authority** or, when feeling old-fashioned, **the Crown**.* Its founding document is not a constitution. It is a *liability framework*. Citizens are not subjects; they are *covered parties*. Crime is not punished; it is *assessed against your premium*. War is theoretically possible but has never occurred, because no underwriter in the Actuariat will write a policy for it, and in Bermudian space an uninsurable act is a legally meaningless one.

> \* There is, somewhere on the Rise, a Governor. There has *always* been a Governor — the post predates the Heave, the spaceflight, and arguably literacy. Nobody is entirely sure what the Governor does, but the office has a flag, a parade, and a budget, so it is allowed to continue.

## DRESS, MANNERS, AND OTHER WAR CRIMES

Because the Bermudian state was assembled out of the surviving institutions of one small island, its culture is — to outside eyes — deranged, and to its own eyes, *correct*.

- **The uniform of the Bermudian space service is Bermuda shorts.** Tailored, pressed, regulation. Knee-high socks, blazer, the works. A fleet admiral commands the deadliest ships humanity has ever built while dressed for a garden party in 1955, and will execute you (actuarially) for laughing.
- **Everything is denominated in the Bermudian dollar**, a currency pegged to nothing in particular and managed by people who consider "pegged to nothing in particular" a sophisticated and deliberate strategy.
- **All correspondence is polite.** Aggressively, terminally polite. The most threatening document a covered party can receive is a letter that opens *"We do hope this finds you well."*

## THE EASTER EGG: THE FIRST EXODUS

When the Rise made spaceflight possible, the very first people off the planet were, predictably, the billionaires.

They did not stay to help. They pooled their fortunes, commissioned a fleet of magnificent self-contained arks, and departed to found a perfect society — a place of pure merit and zero obligation, which they incorporated, with no apparent irony, as a limited liability company and named **Ascendance**.†

Ascendance lasted **fourteen months.**

It turns out that a civilization composed entirely of people who have never personally fixed anything is a civilization with a number of unaddressed structural problems, most of them plumbing. The arks had been designed by people who assumed the toilets, the air recyclers, the hydroponics, the coffee, and the *coffee about the coffee* would simply continue to happen, as they always had, performed by persons whom the founders had specifically declined to bring along. There was no one to do the work. Worse, there was no one to do the work *and then complain about it*, which is, it transpires, the load-bearing emotional infrastructure of any functioning society.

So they came back. Quietly. The official Authority term is **"voluntary repatriation of stranded assets."** And humanity, having watched the experiment fail in real time, drew the one durable lesson of the age and built its second generation of ships properly: ugly, sturdy, over-staffed, and crewed by **people who could actually fly the bloody things.**

The garbage barge **S.V. Dross** is exactly such a ship. Working hull, working crew, working-class to the bolts. It is everything Ascendance refused to be, which is to say: *operational.*

> † The colony was renamed four times for branding reasons before launch — *Excelsior, Meritcorp, Aspiration™,* and finally *Ascendance* — none of which addressed the toilets.

## TYING THE LORE INTO THE *DROSS*

A few threads you can pull during the session:

- **The salvaged pod is from Ascendance.** That derelict escape pod the *Dross* hauled in three weeks ago? It's a founder-class lifeboat from the First Exodus — gold-trimmed, monogrammed, and absolutely riddled with a stowaway nobody bothered to declare on the manifest. The billionaires didn't just leave behind a mess. They left behind **Brood Mama**, who has been raising her family in the warm machinery the way her family rode out the collapse of a perfect society: rent-free, and thriving.

- **The customs checkpoint is Bermudian.** If the crew lets the air run out and the ship drifts (the 0% O₂ "loss" ending), it's impounded at a **Crown Reclamation Checkpoint** by an underwriter in pressed shorts and knee socks who *does hope this finds them well.* The shame is not dying. The shame is the **paperwork.**

- **GLADYS is government-issue.** The ship's senile AI was manufactured to Authority spec, which means she is unfailingly polite, deeply concerned with liability, and legally incapable of telling the crew anything that might constitute "advice." When she says *"there's something in the vending machines again,"* she is not warning them. She is *establishing that she disclosed it.*

- **Dusty's salvage authorization is the smoking gun.** The reason Dusty doesn't remember signing off on the pod salvage: an Ascendance founder-lifeboat is technically a "repatriating stranded asset," and recovering one comes with a finder's bounty *and* a stack of liability the size of a small moon. Someone filled out the form in his name. Following *that* thread is your sequel.

## THE TONE, IN ONE PARAGRAPH

This is a universe that is grimy and lived-in and just on the wrong side of competent — where the machines talk back, the coffee has opinions, and the air supply is a negotiation. But it sits inside a galaxy run with the deadpan, infinite, faintly menacing politeness of a bureaucracy that has priced every possible disaster, including your own, and found it *covered, subject to the usual excess.* Play the small stuff for warmth and the big stuff for the cosmic shrug. The crew of the *Dross* will never save the galaxy. They might, if they're lucky and clever, save *the afternoon* — and get the air back on before the customs forms arrive.
